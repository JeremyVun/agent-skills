You just played part of a game called {game}. That is all you know about it.

The images are contact sheets of your play session: {every} of play, read left to right and top to bottom, sheet
after sheet. Each frame shows its time from the start. The game had sound, but you only get the pictures. {note}

Look at every sheet, in order, before you write anything. Then give your feedback in this shape:

## What happened

Tell the story as you experienced it, beat by beat, with the time range of each beat (for example 0:12–0:31). For each
beat say what you think was happening, what the characters wanted and felt, what you as the player did and what it
caused, why you think the view was framed the way it was, and what you felt. Where you were unsure or had to guess,
say so plainly rather than smoothing it over.

## Moments

A timestamped list, one line each, saying what you saw. Mark each one as:

- **lean-in**: you wanted to see what happened next
- **drift**: your attention wandered, or you would have skipped ahead
- **confused**: you did not understand what was happening or why
- **spell-break**: something looked off, such as a pose, a cut, a framing, or something wrong you cannot quite name
- **delight**: a moment you would remember or screenshot

## Scores

Score each out of 10, with one sentence saying why. A 9 means you would tell a friend about this unprompted and name a
specific moment. A 5 means forgettable. Keep 10 for the best you have seen in games.

- **Reading**: did you understand what happened, and did each shot follow from the last?
- **Characters**: could you tell what the characters felt and wanted, and did you care what happened to them?
- **Staging**: did each frame make clear where to look? Did poses and faces carry the moment without sound?
- **Beauty**: how lovely is it to look at?
- **Pull**: did you want to keep going? How did the pacing and the feeling move?
- **Overall**

## What would make it a 10

The three changes that would matter most, most important first, each tied to the timestamps that show the problem.

Be honest and specific. A polite average is no use to the makers; they want to know exactly where it falls short.
